**Timestamp**: 2026-11-14 09:24:35
**Author**: Claude Code (AI Assistant) & Wut

**Action**: The Home sky map now shows a real SPHEREx picture of the whole sky instead of the drawn Milky Way. Frontend only; no backend or database change.

---

## 1. Summary of Changes
- **Data source**: IRSA's "SPHEREx DR1 all-sky data cube" (`https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/hips/SPHEREx/spherex_allsky_cube`, released 2026-11-01, found through the IRSA link on the challenge's Resources tab). It is a set of greyscale PNG tiles in the HiPS layout, 102 layers. The map uses the first layer (0.75 µm). The browser loads the tiles directly; IRSA allows cross-origin requests, so the backend is not involved.
- **Drawing**: a `<canvas>` sits between the dark oval and the grid. For every map pixel it undoes the map projection (same maths as `inverse()` in `core/sky.js`), finds the HEALPix tile pixel covering that sky position, and colours it from the map's navy to warm white.
- **Detail by zoom**: one 1.1 MB whole-sky preview is loaded first. From about 180% zoom the map also loads 512 px tiles for the visible area (order 1, then order 2 from about 365%), 4 at a time. Zoomed out, the preview is averaged down to calm the speckle.
- **Speed**: while the map is dragged or zoomed it is drawn at half size, then at full size 140 ms after it rests.
- **States**: "Loading the real picture of the sky from NASA…" while the preview downloads; an error with "Try again" if the preview or some sharper tiles fail. There is no drawn stand-in: without the picture the oval stays plain dark with the grid and markers.
- **Removed**: the drawn Milky Way and its file `core/milkyWay.js` (decoration, not data).
- **Unchanged**: drag, zoom, project markers, pin, pointer read-out, cards and controls. `project()` and `inverse()` were not touched. Grid lines and hour labels got lighter colours and a text shadow so they stay readable on the bright Milky Way.

## 4. Known Limits
- The real picture has brightness steps between neighbouring tiles and is speckled when zoomed in. That is how IRSA's tiles look; nothing is smoothed over.
- Only one of the 102 layers is shown, in one colour scale. A colour picture from three layers or a slider through the layers is not built.
- The canvas is 1640 x 780 pixels, so on high-density screens the picture is slightly softer than the text and markers.
- The map now depends on `irsa.ipac.caltech.edu` being reachable from the visitor's browser.
- `CLAUDE.md` needed no change.
